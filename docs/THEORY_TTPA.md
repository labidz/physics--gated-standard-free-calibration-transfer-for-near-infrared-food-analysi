# TTPA v4: identifiability, the mean-shift split, and the significance gates

Notation. Source spectra $x_s\in\mathbb R^p$ (labelled), target spectra $x_t$ (unlabelled), wavelength grid
$\lambda_1<\dots<\lambda_p$. The source chemistry is summarised by its mean $\mu_S$ and the $k$-dimensional
principal subspace $\mathcal V=\operatorname{span}P_S$ ($P_S\in\mathbb R^{p\times k}$ orthonormal). $L\in\mathbb R^{p\times 3}$
spans constant, linear and quadratic baselines.

**Measurement model.** The target instrument (or measurement condition) acts on what the source instrument
would have recorded through a parametric operator $S_\theta$ and an additive response $d$:
$$x_t = S_\theta(\mu_S + P_S c) + L\beta + d + \varepsilon,\qquad
(S_\theta x)(\lambda)=\big(g_\sigma * x\big)\!\Big(\lambda - s - w\,\tfrac{\lambda-\bar\lambda}{h}\Big),\tag{1}$$
with $\theta=(s,w,\sigma)$: axis shift $s$, linear axis warp $w$ (edge displacement), Gaussian line-shape
broadening $\sigma\ge 0$ ($g_\sigma$, applied to the source side); $\bar\lambda,h$ are the grid centre and half-width.
$c$ follows the (possibly shifted) target chemistry distribution, $\beta$ a per-spectrum baseline,
$\varepsilon$ zero-mean noise with covariance $\nu^2 I$.

## 1. The instrument operator is identifiable from the target covariance

Let $\Sigma_t$ be the target covariance and $\mathcal U_t$ its dominant $k$-dimensional eigenspace. Under (1) with
$\operatorname{Cov}(c)\succ 0$ and isotropic noise, $\mathcal U_t\subseteq S_\theta\mathcal V\oplus\operatorname{span}L$.
TTPA estimates $\theta$ by minimising the out-of-subspace energy of the variance-weighted target signal
$U_t$ (rows $= s_j v_j^\top$ from the SVD of the centred target spectra):
$$R(\theta')=\frac{\|U_t(I-\Pi_{\theta'})\|_F^2}{\|U_t\|_F^2},\qquad
\Pi_{\theta'}=\text{orthogonal projector onto }S_{\theta'}\mathcal V\oplus\operatorname{span}L.\tag{2}$$

*Centring makes (2) invariant to the target mean*, so neither an additive instrument offset nor a shift of the
mean chemistry can bias $\hat\theta$ (they only move $\mathbb E\,x_t$).

**Proposition 1 (local identifiability).** In the noise-free population limit $R(\theta)=0$. Define the
transversality matrix $J(\theta)=\big[(I-\Pi_\theta)\,\partial_{\theta_j}S_\theta P_S C^{1/2}\big]_{j=1,2,3}$
(stacked column-wise), with $C=\operatorname{Cov}(c)$. If $J(\theta)$ has full column rank, then
$R(\theta+\delta)=\delta^\top H\delta+o(\|\delta\|^2)$ with $H=J^\top J/\|U_t\|_F^2\succ 0$, so $\theta$ is a strict
local minimiser and locally identifiable; if $J$ is rank deficient, the corresponding directions of $\theta$ are
not identifiable from unlabelled data.

*Proof sketch.* $\Pi_{\theta+\delta}=\Pi_\theta+\sum_j\delta_j\,\partial_j\Pi_\theta+O(\|\delta\|^2)$ and, for a projector
onto the range of $M(\theta)=[S_\theta P_S,L]$, $(I-\Pi)\,\partial_j\Pi\,M = (I-\Pi)\,\partial_j M$. Since
$U_t=M A$ for some coefficient matrix $A$ (population limit), $U_t(I-\Pi_{\theta+\delta})^\top$ has first-order term
$\sum_j\delta_j(I-\Pi_\theta)\partial_j M A$, whose squared Frobenius norm is $\delta^\top(J^\top J)\delta$ after
absorbing $A$ into $C^{1/2}$. $\square$

*Interpretation.* $\partial_s S_\theta x\approx -x'$ (a first-derivative spectrum) and
$\partial_\sigma S_\theta x\approx \sigma x''$ (a second derivative). The shift is identifiable whenever derivative
spectra of the chemistry are not themselves inside the chemical subspace plus a quadratic baseline — i.e. whenever
the spectra carry bands. Flat or nearly linear spectra make $J$ ill-conditioned; the significance gate (Section 3)
then refuses to adapt. The corn instrument pairs give an empirical check: unpaired TTPA shift estimates agree with
shifts fitted from 80 paired spectra across all six pairs ($r=0.995$, mean absolute error 0.14 nm on a 2 nm grid).

## 2. Which part of the mean shift can be corrected without labels

Write the target mean difference after the operator as
$\Delta=\mathbb E\,x_t-S_\theta\mu_S=\Delta_\parallel+\Delta_\perp$ with $\Delta_\parallel=\Pi_\theta\Delta$.

**Proposition 2 (non-identifiability of the in-span mean shift).** For any $v\in S_\theta\mathcal V\oplus\operatorname{span}L$
there is a change of the chemistry/baseline distribution (shift $c\mapsto c+a$, $\beta\mapsto\beta+b$ with
$S_\theta P_S a+Lb=v$) that produces exactly the same distribution of $x_t$ as an instrument response $d=v$
with unchanged chemistry. Hence no procedure that sees only unlabelled target spectra (and the labelled source)
can decide whether $\Delta_\parallel$ is an instrument artefact (to be removed) or a genuine change of composition
(to be kept). $\Delta_\perp$, in contrast, cannot be generated by any composition inside the source chemical span:
it is either an instrument response or a new constituent, and in both cases the source model should not respond to it.

*Proof.* Linearity of $S_\theta$: $S_\theta(\mu_S+P_S(c+a))+L(\beta+b)=S_\theta(\mu_S+P_Sc)+L\beta+v$. $\square$

*Consequence (design of TTPA v4).* $\Delta_\perp$ is always eligible for correction; $\Delta_\parallel$ is corrected only
when the deployment context asserts an unchanged population ("fleet": new instrument/unit or measurement condition,
same product stream). This is the decision a practitioner already makes when choosing between instrument
standardisation and model updating; Proposition 2 shows that it cannot be delegated to unlabelled data. Mean-centring
methods (DMC, di-PLS, CORAL, uDOP's virtual standards) implicitly assume $\Delta_\parallel$ is instrumental and therefore
remove genuine composition changes under population shift — the failure seen for all of them on new seasons,
cultivars and products.

## 3. Significance gates (exact Monte-Carlo tests)

TTPA v4 adapts a component only if the data show it. With $n_a=\min(n_A,\lfloor n_S/2\rfloor)$ and $m=n_S-n_a$, every
test statistic is computed by the same function $T(\mathcal S,\mathcal A)$ of a pseudo-source $\mathcal S$ of size $m$ and
a pseudo-target $\mathcal A$ of size $n_a$ (quick fits from $\theta=0$):
$T=\big(R(0),\,R(\hat s,\hat w),\,R(\hat s,\hat w,\hat\sigma),\,\|\hat\Delta_\perp\|^2/p,\,\|\hat\Delta_\parallel\|^2/p\big)$.
The *real* statistic uses a random size-$m$ subset of the source and a random size-$n_a$ subset of the target;
the $B=19$ *null* statistics use random splits of the source alone. Derived gates:
* operator: $G=\{R(0)-R(\hat s,\hat w)\}/R(0)$ (shift and warp);
* broadening (nested): $G_\sigma=\{R(\hat s,\hat w)-R(\hat s,\hat w,\hat\sigma)\}/R(\hat s,\hat w)$;
* goodness of fit ('chem' context): the post-fit residual must not exceed the largest null post-fit residual;
* out-of-span mean shift $\|\hat\Delta_\perp\|^2$; in-span mean shift $\|\hat\Delta_\parallel\|^2$ ('fleet' context only).
Each component is applied iff its real statistic exceeds all $B$ null values. Estimation of the applied
$\hat\theta,\hat\Delta$ uses all source and target spectra.

**Proposition 3 (size).** Suppose target and source spectra are i.i.d. from one distribution (same instrument,
measurement condition and population). (i) If each null statistic were computed from an independent sample of
size $m+n_a$ from that distribution, the real statistic and the $B$ null statistics would be i.i.d., the rank of the
real one would be uniform on $\{1,\dots,B+1\}$, and $\Pr(T_{\rm real}>\max_b T_b)\le 1/(B+1)=0.05$ exactly.
(ii) The implemented null re-uses the source sample (subsampling without replacement, as in permutation tests
with a reference set); the draws are then identically distributed but weakly dependent through shared samples,
and the size statement holds approximately. Both the pseudo-sources and the real reference have size $m$, which
removes the bias that arises when the real statistic uses all $n_S$ source spectra (in a first version, which
compared a full-source statistic with smaller-source nulls, the false-adaptation rate on same-instrument corn
pseudo-targets was 0.95; with matched sizes it is 0.05-0.10 over 20 draws).

*Proof of (i).* With independent draws of identical size profiles, the $B+1$ statistics are i.i.d.; ties have
probability zero for continuous spectra. $\square$

E12-size verifies the false-adaptation rate empirically on same-instrument pseudo-targets of every development
dataset. Power depends on $m$ and $n_a$; with small source sets (e.g. 40 corn calibration samples) a real but small
instrument shift may go undetected, in which case TTPA v4 falls back to the components that are detected.

**Deployment context** (declared by the user, fixed by the experimental design here):
*fleet* — the instrument, unit or measurement condition changed and the sample population did not: operator
(shift, warp; broadening by the nested test), $\Delta_\perp$ and $\Delta_\parallel$ are all eligible.
*chem* — the population may have changed (new season, cultivar, product, process stage) on the same kind of
measurement. By Proposition 2 the in-span mean shift is not attributable, and a line-shape change cannot be told
apart from band-width changes of new chemistry; in development, operators that passed the improvement and
goodness-of-fit gates were still spurious in population families. The frozen configuration therefore returns the
source model unchanged in this context (the gated-operator version is reported as an ablation, TTPA4-safe).

**Frozen configuration (PROTOCOL Amendment A, "TTPA").** *fleet*: operator applied iff the improvement gate and
the goodness-of-fit gate fire (broadening iff the nested gate fires); $\Delta_\parallel$ always corrected;
$\Delta_\perp$ corrected iff its gate fires. *chem*: no adaptation.

## 4. Forward simulation

Prediction refits the source calibration (same preprocessing and number of latent variables) on
$\tilde x_s=S_{\hat\theta}x_s+\hat d$ and applies it to raw target spectra. If $\hat\theta\to\theta$ and
$\hat d\to d$ (fleet context, Proposition 2 assumption), $(\tilde x_s,y_s)$ has the joint law of a calibration set
measured on the target instrument, so the transferred model inherits the in-domain risk of a target-instrument
calibration built from the source samples — without a single standard or target label. Nothing is projected out of
the target spectra, so no analyte information is discarded (in contrast to orthogonalisation methods such as EPO/uDOP).
